# Product soul

Sits above `VISUAL_PRINCIPLES.md`, `EXPERIENCE_LANGUAGE.md`,
`INTERACTION_LANGUAGE.md` and `VISUAL_IDENTITY.md`. Those documents describe
how the product looks, how it behaves over time, how it responds to being
touched, and why it is recognisable. This one exists to explain why they were
worth writing.

Nothing here is about the interface. If a sentence below could be pasted onto
another product, it has failed and should be rewritten.

---

## 1. Why this deserves to exist

Construction runs on claims that become impossible to check.

Who was on site on the eleventh. Whether that was a full day or a half day.
Whether the sand arrived before or after the pour. Whether the photograph was
taken that morning or found in a gallery three weeks later. Whether the money
handed to a supervisor was spent, returned, or quietly absorbed.

None of these are exotic disputes. They are the ordinary substance of the work,
and they are settled months later, from memory, by whoever's account is more
convincing.

The paper site notebook was never popular because it was pleasant. It was
authoritative because of *when* it was written — at the moment, by the person
who was standing there. Its power was not storage. It was **proximity to the
event**.

Most software destroys exactly that. It lets you enter Tuesday's work on Friday.
It lets you attach any photograph to any day. It lets you correct an
inconvenient number quietly. Each of those is a small convenience, and together
they turn a record into an opinion.

This product exists to take the notebook's authority and keep it — while
removing the notebook's real failures, which were that it could be lost, could
not be added up, and could only be in one place at a time.

**It deserves to exist because it is willing to be less convenient than it could
be, in exchange for producing a record that survives being questioned.**

---

## 2. The uncertainty it removes

Three kinds, and they belong to different people.

**For the person on site: the uncertainty of being doubted.** A supervisor who
records at the moment, with provenance, cannot later be accused of inventing a
day, inflating a count, or losing money. The controls that look like policing
are the same controls that make them unaccusable. This is the most
misunderstood thing about the product and the most important: *evidence protects
the person who produced it first, and the office second.*

**For the person paying: the uncertainty of what is actually theirs.** Money
sits in an account that is not all yours. Tax collected on someone else's behalf
is in there. Charges owed onward are in there. A balance is not a position, and
the distance between the two is exactly the distance between paying wages and
borrowing from an obligation without noticing.

**For everyone: the uncertainty of what already happened.** Not forecasts. Not
projections. The far more ordinary and more corrosive question of what was
already true yesterday, which in this industry is genuinely hard to answer.

The product does not predict. It settles the past so that the present can be
acted on.

---

## 3. After a year

Not "efficient". Not "in control", which is a word for people who were never
really at risk.

The feeling should be: **believed.**

A contractor who has used this for a year should find that arguments end
earlier. That a client's query is answered with a record rather than a
recollection. That a subcontractor's claim can be checked without a row. That
when they say a number, the room accepts it — because their numbers have been
right before, and because they can show why.

The product's real output is not a dashboard. It is **credibility**, accumulated
slowly, in an industry where credibility is the actual working capital.

A quieter version of the same feeling, for the supervisor: a year of never once
having to defend a memory.

---

## 4. What is never sacrificed

**The record's proximity to the event.** Nothing may make it easier to enter
yesterday's truth tomorrow. The window is a feature. If it becomes friction
users complain about, the answer is to make recording faster at the moment — not
to widen the window.

**The distinction between money held and money owned.** No screen may ever
present an obligation as an asset, however much better the figure would look.

**Readability by someone who did not choose this software.** A worker checking
what they are owed, a supervisor reading in sunlight, someone more comfortable
in Gujarati than English. The person who bought the product is not the person
whose comprehension matters most.

**The refusal to be flattering.** A bad month must look like a bad month. A
product that softens numbers to feel pleasant has quietly become a report
written for the owner rather than a record written for the truth.

**The constraint applying to everyone.** Controls that bind the supervisor but
bend for the administrator are not controls. They are theatre, and everyone on
site will know it within a week.

---

## 5. What remains after closing it

Not a screen. A settled feeling that **nothing is outstanding that you do not
know about.**

The specific relief of having finished looking. Most business software leaves a
residue of suspicion — a sense that something is buried a few clicks deeper, in
a report you did not run. This product should end that. When you close it, the
question is closed too.

If a user thinks about the product at all after closing it, the thought should
be: *nothing is waiting for me that I have not seen.*

---

## 6. Why this is not those other products

Set appearance aside entirely.

Every product in that comparison set is built on an assumption this one cannot
make: **that the record is uncontested.**

When you write a document, plan an issue, or move money over a payment rail, the
system is the sole author and the ledger is authoritative because the rail is
authoritative. Nobody will later dispute whether the transaction occurred. The
job of that software is to remove friction between intention and result.

Here, the money moved as cash, on a site, between people, and the record exists
only because someone chose to make it. It *will* be disputed — by a client, a
subcontractor, a tax authority, or simply by memory. The system is not the
author. It is the **witness**.

That single difference inverts the design brief. Friction is not automatically
waste; some friction is what makes the witness credible. Speed is not
automatically a virtue; speed that permits a careless record is a liability.
Convenience is not automatically good; convenience for the powerful user at the
expense of the record is corruption.

Those products optimise the distance between a person and their intention. This
one optimises the distance between an event and its record — and then defends
that record from everybody, including the person who owns the company.

---

## 7. Worldview

**On work.** Work happens where the dust is, not where the desk is. The office
does not perform the work; it depends on it. Software that treats site input as
inconvenient raw material for office reporting has the relationship backwards.

**On information.** Information has a birthplace and a birth time, and its value
decays as it moves away from both. A figure re-keyed in an office on Friday is a
different and weaker object than the same figure captured on site on Tuesday,
even when the digits match.

**On finance.** Money in an account is a fact. Money you may spend is a
conclusion, and it is always smaller. Any system that shows the first and lets
the user infer the second is participating in the mistake.

**On certainty.** Certainty is not confidence. It is the ability to show your
working. The product should make people *less* willing to assert things they
cannot support — and far more comfortable asserting the things they can.

**On attention.** Attention is finite and mostly already spent. It should be
asked for only when something is genuinely contested, unresolved, or late.
Everything that is fine should be visibly, checkably fine — and should then be
left alone.

---

## 8. Signature moments

Experiences, not interactions. Every future decision should strengthen at least
one, and none may weaken any.

**"I recorded it before I left."**
The event and its record are separated by minutes, not by an evening of
remembering.

**"I can show why."**
A question does not become an argument, because the answer includes its
evidence.

**"I know what is actually mine."**
The obligations are already deducted. There is no second calculation waiting to
disappoint anybody.

**"Nobody had to be trusted."**
Not the supervisor, not the office, not the owner. The record does not rest on
anyone's good character — which is precisely what makes it safe for everyone's
good character.

**"I stopped looking, and I was finished."**
Not the feeling of having seen a lot. The feeling of having seen everything that
was waiting.

---

## 9. Anti-goals

The product refuses to become:

- **A surveillance tool.** The controls exist to make a record defensible, not
  to catch people. The moment a control's primary purpose is suspicion of the
  workforce, it has become something else and should be removed.
- **A flattering mirror for the owner.** No figure is chosen, framed or softened
  because it makes the business look better to the person reading it.
- **A reporting product.** Reports summarise the past for someone who was
  absent. This is for people who were present and must now act.
- **A forecasting product.** It has no honest basis for prediction, and pretending
  otherwise would trade the one thing it does have — evidentiary credibility —
  for the appearance of insight.
- **A general-purpose ledger.** Its opinions about half-days, floats, provenance
  and windows are the product. Generalising them away leaves a spreadsheet.
- **Convenient at the record's expense.** Every request to make entry easier is
  legitimate; every request to make it *later* is not.

---

## 10. Laws

Immutable. If a future decision conflicts with one of these, the decision is
wrong, not the law.

1. **The record belongs to the moment it describes.** Distance from the event is
   the only thing that reliably destroys truth.
2. **Constraints bind everyone or they bind no one.** A rule the owner can
   bypass is not a rule; it is an inconvenience for subordinates.
3. **Money held is never shown as money owned.**
4. **Evidence protects its author first.** Any control that fails this test is
   policing, and policing is not the product.
5. **Friction that protects the record is craft. Friction that protects nothing
   is a defect.** The two are told apart by asking what would be lost if it were
   removed.
6. **Ask for attention only when something is contested, unresolved, or late.**
   Everything else earns silence.
7. **The product must be readable by the person with the least reason to have
   learned it.**
8. **Never soften a number.** The one thing this product cannot survive is being
   caught being kind about the truth.

## 11. The user

Not a demographic. A mental state.

**The one on site** is standing up, holding a phone in one hand, in strong
light, with somebody waiting for them. They are interrupted every few minutes
and have been since morning. They are not resistant to recording things; they
are resistant to *stopping* to record things. Their private fear is not
complexity — it is being blamed later for a discrepancy they cannot reconstruct,
in a conversation where their memory is the only evidence they have. They need
software that costs seconds, forgives nothing about timing, and then stands
beside them when the question comes.

**The one in the office** is holding several incompatible obligations at once:
wages that are due, a client who has not paid, tax that is not theirs sitting in
an account that looks healthy. They are not short of numbers. They are short of
the *one* number that accounts for everything they owe before it tells them what
they have. Their fear is a specific, quiet one: discovering in month four that
something has been wrong since month one, and having paid people out of money
that was never available.

Both are under time pressure from other people, not from themselves. Neither
opened this to explore. They came for an answer and they intend to leave.

## 12. Voice

The product speaks like a good site engineer with nothing to prove.

**Plain.** It states what is true and stops. It does not narrate its own
correctness.

**Unhurried.** Nothing is urgent unless it is genuinely late. It never
manufactures pressure to seem important.

**Unapologetic.** When it blocks a backdated entry, it says what the rule is. It
does not apologise for the rule, soften it, or imply an exception might be
available. A rule that sounds negotiable is already broken.

**Uncongratulatory.** Recording a day's work is not an achievement. Nothing is
celebrated for having merely happened.

**Precise about doubt.** Where it does not know, it says what it does know
instead — the date it can prove rather than the date it could infer. It never
fills a gap with a plausible guess, because a plausible guess is the exact
substance of the disputes it exists to end.

It never speaks to the owner differently than to the supervisor.

## 13. Sources

**Adopted.** `PRODUCT.md` is the origin of everything above, and it is the
highest-priority source. Its statement that the product *"treats site-recorded
data as evidence rather than as data entry"*, and its list of untouchable
controls — the two-day rule, photo provenance, the labour ledger, supervisor
float — are not features. They are the product's convictions expressed as
mechanism, and this document is largely an attempt to say out loud what they
already imply. Its priority-persona order, which puts the site supervisor above
the administrator, is the source of Law 7 and of §12's final sentence.

**Deliberately not repeated.** The numeral system, the elevation rule, the
lighting temperature and the material and type systems belong to
`VISUAL_IDENTITY.md`; the laws they obey belong to `VISUAL_PRINCIPLES.md`.
Where this document touches money or attention, it states the belief only;
every expression of it lives downstream.

**Design skills — not consulted, deliberately.** Taste, Impeccable, UI/UX Pro
Max, 21st.dev and Emil Kowalski's motion principles are all craft sources. They
answer *how well* something is made, never *why it should exist*. Consulting
them here would have produced a more polished document about nothing. They are
correctly applied one level down, and the adopt/reject ledger for them already
exists in `VISUAL_IDENTITY.md`.

**External research — none, and none needed.** Every question this document asks
was answerable from `PRODUCT.md` and the domain constraints already recorded
there. Reaching outward would have imported someone else's convictions, which is
the one thing a soul document cannot survive.
